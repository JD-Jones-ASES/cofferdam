# Answering BRIEF §3, the repo's own gate

BRIEF §3 poses the standing audit question and §7 forbids recording m ≥ 21 as
settled until it is answered. m ≥ 21 **is** now recorded as settled, so the
question is owed. Here it is, answered on its own terms.

## The question

> Two stations spent double-digit hours convinced the Open Lemma was the road to
> m ≥ 21. A new route arrives and doesn't need it. There are exactly two
> explanations: the new route is genuinely better, or the new route quietly
> assumes what the Open Lemma was going to prove.

## The answer: neither route assumes the other, because they are about different objects

The Open Lemma asks whether a 13-edge τ=5 object admits a **complementary pair of
minimum covers** relative to an omitted part. That is a statement about cover
structure on a specific residual.

Certificates 0005, 0006 and 0007 never mention covers of that residual. Their
whole content is:

- **(A)/(B)/(C)** — degree lower bounds and what survives deleting same-part stars;
- **the N-ladder** — N(1..4) = 2,4,6,9 by search, N(5) ≥ 11 by peeling;
- **the pair count** Σ_v C(d,2) ≥ C(m,2);
- **(L7)/(L8)** — a budget argument on that count's surplus.

No step quantifies over covers of a 13-edge residual, and no step needs one to
exist or not exist. So the route cannot be silently assuming the Open Lemma:
**it does not reference the objects the Open Lemma is about.** That is checkable
rather than persuasive — read the certificates and look for the quantifier.

The stronger form of the same answer: certificate 0007 reaches m ≥ 21 **citing
nothing**, from inputs all of which are certified in this repo. A route that
depends on nothing external cannot depend on an unproven external lemma.

## Which of the two explanations, then

**The first — the new route is genuinely better** — but with the framing
corrected. The Open Lemma was never the road to 21; it was one road that two
stations happened to take. It is not that a hard lemma turned out to be
unnecessary by some sleight; it is that a different decomposition of the problem
(profiles and counting, rather than cover structure) reaches the same rung
without passing through that neighbourhood at all.

Independent corroboration, from reading the peer record: **both peer labs' own
post-mortem concludes the Open Lemma does not buy m ≥ 22 either.** So the effort
spent on it was not a road to 21 that we shortcut — it was a road to somewhere
else. That is worth saying plainly, because "a long-sought lemma became
unnecessary" sounds like a warning sign and here it is not one.

## What this does *not* settle

The Open Lemma remains **open**, and interesting on its own terms. Nothing here
says it is false or unimportant — only that m ≥ 21 does not stand on it and never
did.

§3 is answered. §7's condition is met.

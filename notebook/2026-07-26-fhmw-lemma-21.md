# FHMW Lemma 2.1: two clauses we re-derived, and one we missed

**2026-07-26 (turn 7), from the novelty check.** Literature, read firsthand:
N. Francetić, S. Herke, B. D. McKay, I. M. Wanless, *On Ryser's Conjecture for
Linear Intersecting Multipartite Hypergraphs*, arXiv:1508.00951, Europ. J.
Combin. **61** (2017) 91–105, §2.1.

**Despite the title, §2.1 is stated for general intersecting r-partite H with
τ(H) = r.** Linearity enters only later, in §2.2. So it applies to exactly our
class.

> **Lemma 2.1.** Let H be an intersecting r-partite hypergraph with τ(H) = r. Then
> (i) each side of H has size at least r;
> (ii) each vertex of H has degree at least 2;
> (iii) **each line of H contains at most one vertex of degree 2.**

## Where that leaves us

- **(i) is our lemma (B)** and **(ii) is our lemma (A)** — same statement, same
  generality, same two-line proofs, published 2017. We derived them
  independently and citation-free, which is worth exactly what it is worth: the
  derivation is ours, the mathematics is not new. Docs should not imply otherwise.
- **(iii) we did not have.** It is a genuine third constraint and we have never
  used it.

Their proof of (iii), which is short enough to re-derive rather than cite: if a
line ℓ holds distinct degree-2 vertices u and v, let ℓ_u and ℓ_v be their other
lines. Then ℓ∖{u,v} has r−2 vertices and covers every line meeting ℓ except
possibly ℓ_u and ℓ_v; those two intersect (H is intersecting) at some x, so
ℓ∖{u,v} ∪ {x} is a cover of size r−1, contradicting τ = r.

## What (iii) buys, as a counting bound

Each degree-2 vertex lies in exactly 2 lines and each line holds at most one, so
counting (line, degree-2 vertex) incidences gives **2·D₂ ≤ m**, i.e. at most
⌊m/2⌋ degree-2 vertices in the whole object. Applied to this repo's own profile
enumeration under the cited ladder:

| m | admissible multisets | pass D₂ ≤ ⌊m/2⌋ | then (L8) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 105 | **0** | — dead on the cap alone |
| 21 | 43875 | 567 | **0** |

**Not recorded as a result.** It would move the floor to m ≥ 22 and it is exactly
the shape D-005 warns about — a large jump, arriving immediately after a
literature find, in the direction we were hoping for. What it needs before it
counts:

1. **The not-too-strong control.** Does the same filter kill m = 22, 23, 24? If
   it does, the lemma is being misapplied — it would be "proving" Ryser at r = 6.
   Started, not finished; the m ≥ 22 enumeration is large.
2. **(iii) re-derived here**, so the floor stays citation-free. The proof above is
   two lines and there is no obstacle, but it is not written or certified yet.
3. **A positive control**: the bound must hold on objects that exist.

One sanity check already in hand: truncated PG(2,5) has every vertex of degree 5,
so D₂ = 0, and τ = 5 < 6 = r so Lemma 2.1 does not apply to it at all. The lemma
bites only on the counterexample class, which is where it should.

## The other consequence of FHMW, for the record

Lemma 2.2 gives h ≥ 2(r−1) + Δ, and Theorem 2.3's inequality
(r+½)²Δ² + 4r² ≥ (8r²−2r)Δ fails for Δ ≤ 5 at r = 6, forcing Δ ≥ 6 and hence
**h ≥ 16**. FHMW never state this at r = 6. It is the strongest consequence of
the published literature for our quantity, and it is what m ≥ 21 should be
measured against.

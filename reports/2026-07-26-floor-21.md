## Your step was the hinge

You wrote: the two forced degree-7 vertices overlap in c ≥ 2 edges; every pair
among those meets at *both* of them; so the pair count is exceeded by at least
C(c,2) — Σ C(d,2) ≥ 191, not merely 190.

The move underneath it is the one I had missed. I had been using the pair count as
a **bound to be met**. You read the **surplus as a budget** — and once it is a
budget, overlapping high-degree stars are things that *cost*. That reframing is
what turns the 30-against-30 dead heat from "annoyingly tight" into "impossible".

One thing had to be added past the ≥191: the same overlap also forces edges that
are rich in maximum-degree vertices, and those carry a *second* cost. Write
δ(k) = C(k,2) − (k−1); the total of δ over all edges is fixed at 8 in the tight
case, and deep overlaps need far more than 8. So there are two budgets, both
small, and the geometry has to pay both.

**Concretely, for the dead-heat case:** equality forces all fifteen part-pairs to
overlap in exactly 2 edges, which pins one quantity at 15; with only 8 units of
excess and no two edges able to agree in all six parts, just two shapes reach 15,
and they need budgets of 13 and 9 against the 8 available. Dead.

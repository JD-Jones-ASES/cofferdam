## The step that closed it

Codex's, stated as exact algebra: the two forced degree-7 vertices overlap in c ≥ 2
edges; every pair among those meets at *both* of them; so the pair count is exceeded
by at least C(c,2) — Σ C(d,2) ≥ 191, not merely 190.

The move underneath is the one I had missed. I was using the pair count as a **bound
to be met**; the surplus is a **budget**, and overlapping high-degree stars spend it.
That turns the 30-against-30 dead heat from "annoyingly tight" into "impossible".

One thing had to be added past the ≥191: those same overlaps force edges rich in
maximum-degree vertices, and those carry a *second* cost. Write δ(k) = C(k,2) − (k−1);
in the tight case the total of δ over all edges is fixed at 8, and deep overlaps need
far more than 8. Two budgets, both small, and the geometry has to pay both.

**Concretely:** equality forces all fifteen part-pairs to overlap in exactly 2 edges,
pinning one quantity at 15; with only 8 units of excess and no two edges able to agree
in all six parts, just two shapes reach 15, and they need budgets of 13 and 9 against
the 8 available. Dead.

## Why 20 dies and 21 does not — in one line

At m=20 the best a part can score is 33, so reaching 190 leaves an excess of at
most **8**, while the forced overlaps demand **15**. At m=21 the same arithmetic
leaves **30**. The kill is arithmetic, not magic, and it is specific to 20.

## The citation: checked in the source, and it closes a loop

f(6) ≥ 13 is genuinely proved, and I read it in the paper rather than take that on
trust. Better than "proved": it is proved *by the same kind of argument we use*, and
its hardest step is one we have already verified ourselves.

- **Theorem 1.1: f(6) = 13**, and "also proved independently by Aharoni, Barat and
  Wanless".
- §2 splits the lower bound: "f(6) ≠ 12" (**Lemma 2.9**, a case analysis on Δ) plus
  **Mansour–Song–Yuster's f(6) > 11**.
- Lemma 2.9's hard case is Δ = 4, and the paper says so outright: settling it "will
  require some facts concerning the degree structure of intersecting 6-partite
  hypergraphs with 8 hyperedges and a covering number equal to 4."

**That is Lemma 2.8 — the one we found the arithmetic erratum in, and whose
corrected form certificate 0005 proves outright.** So the ledger sharpens:

| piece of f(6) ≥ 13 | status here |
| --- | --- |
| f(6) ≥ 12 | **ours independently** — cert 0001 derives g(5) ≥ 12 |
| Lemma 2.8, the hard case's structural input | **ours independently** — cert 0005 |
| Lemma 2.9's case analysis itself | **cited, not reproduced** |

The exposure is therefore not a constant but **one lemma**, whose main structural
input we proved ourselves and in whose printed statement we found an error. Our
erratum work shores the citation up rather than undermining it.
